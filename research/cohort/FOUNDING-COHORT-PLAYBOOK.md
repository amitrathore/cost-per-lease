# Founding CPL Benchmark Cohort Playbook

**Program length:** 12 weeks
**Target:** 10 firms, approximately 50 eligible properties, up to 8 quarters each
**Exchange:** approved aggregate data contribution for a confidential assessment; no fee

## 1. Program promise

Each participant receives a private assessment of its eligible properties and a
leave-one-participant-out peer comparison. Cost Per Lease never sells, licenses,
or shares participant- or property-level data. Restricted source and normalized
data are destroyed after report acceptance. Only aggregate statistics that pass
the suppression rules may be retained or published.

## 2. Roles

- **Program owner:** owns schedule, participant relationships, approvals, and
  separation from Leaser AI.
- **Research lead:** owns definitions, mapping decisions, analysis, and reports.
- **Independent reviewer:** re-performs key checks and approves exceptions.
- **Security custodian:** provisions access, maintains the access log, and executes
  deletion.
- **External reviewers:** one multifamily operator and one finance/accounting
  reviewer challenge the methodology before publication.
- **Participant data owner:** confirms authority, source definitions, mappings,
  and final factual accuracy.

One person may hold multiple internal roles during the pilot, but no private
report is released without a second-person calculation review.

## 3. Twelve-week sequence

### Weeks 1–2: Foundation

1. Form or designate the independent Cost Per Lease entity.
2. Obtain counsel review of the contribution agreement, privacy promise,
   destruction schedule, and trademark posture.
3. Select an encrypted, access-controlled file-transfer/data-room provider.
4. Lock Standard v0.1-draft, the data dictionary, calculation spec, and quality
   controls for the pilot.
5. Create a synthetic eight-quarter property submission and expected results.
6. Appoint external methodology reviewers.

**Exit gate:** counsel-approved terms, secure transfer tested, synthetic model
passes, no open definition that changes an input or denominator.

### Weeks 3–5: Recruit and contract

1. Build a list of 25–30 candidate firms to obtain 10 participants.
2. Send the invitation and one-page cohort brief.
3. Hold a 30-minute fit call using the qualification checklist.
4. Confirm 3–8 eligible properties per firm and a named data owner.
5. Execute the contribution agreement before accepting any data.
6. Assign random firm and property codes; store the identity mapping separately.

**Exit gate:** 10 signed firms or a documented decision to proceed with a pilot.

### Weeks 4–7: Pilot and intake

1. Onboard two pilot firms first.
2. Walk through the intake workbook without requesting raw system exports.
3. Scan the uploaded workbook for hidden sheets, external links, unexpected fields,
   and prohibited data before analysis.
4. Run a validation interview and return a written discrepancy list.
5. Revise instructions only if the change does not alter locked definitions. Log
   all revisions.
6. After both pilots pass, open intake to the remaining participants.

**Exit gate:** two complete, validated pilot submissions and a frozen template.

### Weeks 6–9: Normalize and analyze

1. Preserve the uploaded file as read-only restricted source data.
2. Map each source field to the canonical dictionary in a separate working file.
3. Record every reclassification and allocation decision.
4. Run intake, reconciliation, formula, and duplicate checks.
5. Calculate quarterly and TTM metrics.
6. Assign confidence grades.
7. Build leave-one-participant-out peer sets.
8. Independently review inclusion, calculations, exceptions, and outputs.

**Exit gate:** every included property has a signed validation record and second
review; unresolved properties are excluded rather than estimated silently.

### Weeks 9–10: Private assessments

1. Prepare each confidential report from the approved template.
2. Hold a 60-minute readout focused on cost composition and decision implications.
3. Give the participant five business days to correct factual mapping errors.
4. Freeze the accepted report and record the acceptance date.
5. Ask whether the assessment revealed a previously unmeasured cost, changed a
   decision, and merits a repeat next year.

**Exit gate:** participant factual confirmation and final research approval.

### Weeks 11–12: Destruction and publication

1. Execute deletion within 30 days after each report's acceptance.
2. Complete and countersign the destruction certificate.
3. Apply suppression rules before retaining any aggregate table.
4. Run owner-concentration sensitivity.
5. Obtain legal and external methodology approval.
6. Publish Standard v0.1 and either:
   - a Founding CPL Benchmark Brief if all gates pass; or
   - a Founding Cohort Methodology Pilot if they do not.

## 4. Participant qualification

Include a firm only when it can provide:

- stabilized, conventional, market-rate multifamily properties;
- at least four consecutive quarters, preferably eight;
- property-level expense and lease-event aggregates;
- a person authorized to contribute the data;
- a finance or accounting contact for reconciliation;
- agreement to the definitions, restricted-use terms, and correction window.

Exclude properties in active lease-up or material repositioning, or analyze them
privately without adding them to the peer cohort.

## 5. Communication cadence

- Day 0: signed agreement and welcome note.
- Day 2: 45-minute intake orientation.
- Day 14: target submission.
- Day 17: discrepancy list.
- Day 21: validation interview.
- Day 35: target calculation approval.
- Day 42: private readout.
- Day 47: factual correction deadline.
- Within 30 days after acceptance: destruction confirmation.

## 6. Program dashboard

Track only operational metadata outside restricted storage:

- firm code, agreement status, data owner, property count;
- orientation, submission, validation, report, acceptance, and deletion dates;
- issue count and status without confidential values;
- repeat-assessment commitment;
- publication eligibility status.

Do not place property financial values in the program dashboard.

## 7. Success criteria

- 10 signed firms.
- 50 properties submitted; at least 40 pass validation.
- At least 80 percent of participants report a newly visible cost or clarified
  decision.
- At least seven firms commit to repeat participation.
- Two independent reviewers reproduce the synthetic expected results.
- No restricted source or normalized record remains after its deadline.

## 8. Stop conditions

Pause intake immediately after an unauthorized disclosure, prohibited-data upload,
unexplained access event, material formula error, or inability to meet a deletion
commitment. Notify affected participants and counsel before resuming.
